## [Unreleased]

- Your data as "This work" (issue 39, first part): `Publication(; label = "This work", title, authors, year, journal, doi, note)` describes the article that your data will be published in, and `set_publication!(db, publication)` gives it to the databases of kind `:user` (`database = "name"` to one only, `nothing` to take it away; `publication(db)` returns it). It is kept in the description file of a saved database, and setting it again (with the DOI, once there is one) replaces it
  - `table` of a selection of the database has the new column `work` (the label for the points of the databases of kind `:user`, "Literature" for the others) when the database has a publication; `this_work = false` leaves it out and `this_work = true` or a `Publication` asks for it without setting it. The column `source` keeps its meaning (the database of the reference)
  - The figures draw all the points of the user as one group of their own, the label of the publication in the legend, with the highlight colour and marker of the style (`PlotStyle.highlight`, `highlight_marker`; a star) on top of the others, whatever `marker_by` is; `marker_by = :work` puts the literature together against it

- Your data as "This work" (issue 39, second part): exports of a selection of several databases, and how the data of the user are referred to
  - `Publication(...; cite = :label | :unpublished | :published)` (default `:label`; `:published` needs the `doi`) says how the exports refer to your data, and `cite = ...` in `export_points`, `write_bibtex`, `bibtex`, `write_references` and `reference_summary` chooses again for that call. `:label` writes "This work" and no entry in the `.bib` (for the paper that publishes the data), `:unpublished` writes the key of an `@unpublished` entry built from the fields of the publication (for reports) and `:published` the entry of the DOI from the resolver, completed with those fields. `this_work = false` ignores the publication
  - `export_points`, `write_bibtex`, `bibtex`, `write_references` and `reference_summary` take a selection of several databases (before, only a selection of one): the points of each database are appended in the same file (the header once), the articles are one list with global keys (the same DOI is one entry), and all the points of the databases of kind `:user` are one row for each property of the references table. The ThermoML formats are refused for several databases
  - In a LaTeX table a label ("This work") is text and not a `\cite`
  - `move_reference!(db, reference, to; database, metadata, on_conflict = :error, confirm = false)` moves a reference, with its experiments, points, samples, conditions and uncertainties, from one database of yours to another; the databases stay apart (it is a copy into `to` and a removal from the origin, each a new dataset, so a mistake leaves both as they were) and the ThermoML archive is never an origin or a destination. Without `confirm = true` it only says what would move. `metadata` changes the DOI, title, journal, year, authors... on the way, and `on_conflict = :merge` joins the experiments to a reference of `to` that has the same DOI
  - `publish!(db; doi, to, confirm = false)` is the whole flow for your data once the article is out: every reference of the databases of kind `:user` becomes the article of the DOI (the fields from the resolver, completed with the publication), is moved to the database of literature `to`, and the publication is removed

- Your data as "This work" (issue 39, third part): several works at once, names for experiments, and publishing only some of the data
  - An experiment can have several names. `add_name!(view, name, index = nothing)` adds one to the experiments of a selection (any database, the ThermoML archive too: its names are kept in a file beside the archive, not in it) and keeps the others; `modify_name!(db, old, new; confirm)` changes a name everywhere (the publications follow it) and `modify_name!(view, old, new, index; confirm)` only in the selection; `remove_name!(view, names | true, index; confirm)` takes some or all away; a name that a publication cites cannot vanish. Without `confirm = true` the last two only say what would change. `experiment_names(view)` lists the names of each point. An experiment added to a database of yours is named `<database>_yyyy_mm_dd_HH:MM` (plus `_<compound>` for one pure substance) unless `name` is given. The names stay with the experiment when it moves to another database
  - `points(db; experiment = "name" | r"regex" | [names])` selects by name; an unknown name is an `ArgumentError`
  - `add_publication!(db, Publication(; name = "paper-A", experiments = [...]))`, `add_to_publication!`, `remove_from_publication!`, `remove_publication!` and `publications(db)` prepare several articles at the same time; `this_work` of a table, a figure or an export takes the name of a publication or a selection of points, and only those points are "This work" (the other points of your databases are "Own data"). With several publications `this_work = :auto` is refused by the exports (say which) but not by `table`
  - `move_experiments!(db, names_or_selection, to; metadata, on_conflict, confirm)` moves experiments (only the selected points of a partly selected experiment), and `publish!(db, "paper-A" | selection; doi, to, confirm)` publishes just those, leaving the rest of the notebook; `publish!(db; doi)` still publishes all the data of the user
  - The printed views and tables say how many points were left out as duplicates, and how to see them all
  - The figures take `this_work` (the name of a publication, a selection or `false`), through `plotdata`, to say which points are "This work"
  - `plot_xpt` takes `xscale` and `yscale` (`:linear` or `:log10`); with `color = :count` the cells are equal in the logarithm and the points of "This work" are drawn over the map. The points of "This work" are drawn in the colour of the highlight, also when the others are coloured by their values, and larger (1.9 times the size of the markers)
  - The tutorial "Adding your own data" is now "Normal use for a researcher": the whole path of an experimentalist, with the reason of each step (a look for a gap in the literature with `plot_xpt`, creating databases, adding data by code in the units of the bench (°C, bar) and by spreadsheet, with pictures of the template, editing, names, two articles in preparation, the references table, the figures of "This work" in other units and `publish!`)

## [0.1.3] - 2026-11-22

- Figures (issue 43, first part): the core that does not need a plotting package. `plotdata(selection; ...)` prepares the points once (the columns of `table`, with the exact units of `unit`, `temperature_unit` and `pressure_unit`) and every figure takes it, or the selection itself with the same filters as `points`; a model is a function of the conditions in SI (`model(p, T)`, or `args = (:temperature,)`, `(:temperature, :x1)`) that returns the value in the unit of the data, or the calculated values are given as `calc`, and it runs once for the same conditions
  - `plot_comparison` (values and deviations), `plot_isotherms`, `plot_isobars`, `plot_parity` (bands of the deviation of your choice), `plot_residuals` (violin with the points and the quartiles, box or both; a group with fewer than 10 points gets a box), `plot_xpt` (plane of temperature and pressure coloured by the value, by count, with the references to highlight as stars), `plot_facets` and `plot_by_reference` (panels tagged (a), (b), ... with one colour bar). Each builds a `FigureSpec` (`spec = true` returns it) that the extension of a backend draws with `render`
  - Axes are labelled in LaTeX with the symbol of the property (`\rho`, `c_p`, ...; `register_symbol!` changes one) and the unit in the format of `unitformat` (`:square` by default, `:round`, `:slash` or a function); the markers tell the references apart (`marker_by`), filled and then open
  - Deviations (`DeviationSpec`, `deviation`): `:relative` (default, in percent; also fraction, per mille, ppm and ppb), `:log`, `:smape` (the usual one, without sign), the signed smooth errors `:smae` and `:srl`, and `:absolute`, each with `absolute_value = true` for its absolute value
  - `plot_style(:default)` (scientific colour maps: `managua_r`, `vik`, `batlowS`) and `plot_style(:neon)` (the colours of the documentation, for a dark panel); `plot_defaults(...; persist = true)` sets and keeps the defaults with Preferences.jl. The figures of the vapour–liquid equilibrium and the second cycle of figures come next
  - Makie (`using CairoMakie`, `GLMakie` or `WGLMakie`) and Plots (`using Plots`) draw the figures through extensions: `plot_*` returns a `Figure` (Makie) or a `Plots.Plot`; with both loaded `backend = :makie | :plots` (or `plot_defaults(backend = ...)`) chooses. In Makie, `plot_*!(fig[i, j], data; ...)` fills a position of your own layout and returns the axes; in Plots, `plot(data; kind = :parity, options = (...))` is a recipe that draws the figure of `kind`. LaTeXStrings.jl is now a dependency (the labels are `LaTeXString`s)
  - Vapour–liquid equilibrium and saturation: `equilibrium(data)` puts the rows of the archive together as `EquilibriumPoints` (bubble pressure or boiling temperature with the liquid composition, the vapour composition when the reference gives it, and which of T or p is fixed), `plot_pxy` and `plot_txy` (a panel for each isotherm or isobar, filled for the bubble curve `x` and open for the dew curve `y`, with an optional model curve `model(T, x) -> (p, y)` or `model(p, x) -> (T, y)` in SI), `plot_xy` (vapour against liquid composition, coloured by T or p) and `plot_saturation` (density against temperature, liquid filled and gas open, with the critical point of each reference, and `ln p` against `1000/T`); each has its `plot_*!` form for a position of your own layout
  - Second cycle of figures: `plot_deviation_map` (the deviation of a model in the plane of two conditions, with a colour map centred on zero), `plot_excess` (one panel for each excess property against the mole fraction), `plot_ternary` (three components in a triangle, with the points coloured by a variable) and `plot_years` (points of each year and the cumulative number). The layers have a `:text` kind and a panel can hide its axes (`frame = false`). `plot_pxy` and `plot_txy` without `at` draw the `maxpanels` sections with most points when there are more, and say so
  - Units of the figures: `xunit`, `yunit`, `cunit` and the units of `plotdata` accept text or a unit of Unitful or FlexUnits (read as text, so the conversion is exact and the axes and colour bars are labelled with it); the quantities of DynamicQuantities keep no text of their units and are refused with a message that asks for text
  - The manual has a page of figures (`90-figures.md`) drawn with CairoMakie and GR in the style `:neon` of the documentation, with the colour map of the package (`managua_r`) in the figures of the default style

- Listing the properties (issue 53): `measured_properties(source; search)` lists the properties that a dataset, a selection or a database have as the columns `property` and `points` (most frequent first), without the points. `search` is text that the label contains (any case), a `Regex`, a function of the label or a vector of any of these; the result, or its `property` column, can be given to the keyword `property` of `points` to select all of them at once
- Phase of your own points and saturation (issue 41): the phase of a point stays the text you give, and the ThermoML formats of `export_points` translate it. `Liquid (saturated)` is a `Liquid` property in a block of data that has `Liquid` and `Gas`, `Vapor (saturated)` is a `Gas` property with `Gas` and `Liquid`, and `Liquid + Vapor (saturated)` is a property of the two phases, written for the liquid as the archive does for the vapor pressure; `Vapor` and `Vapour` are `Gas`, and any other text must be a phase of ThermoML (any case). A phase with no equivalent is refused before anything is written, with the number of points and the closest phases of the standard. The text of the user is kept in the file as `sPhaseDescription`, so a reader sees that a point without a pressure is at saturation and not at atmospheric pressure
  - `thermoml_phases()` lists the 33 phases of the standard, and `thermoml_phase(text)` says what a text becomes (`PhaseMeaning`: the phase of the property, the phases of the block, whether it is saturated and the description). The archive's `Metastable liquid`, which the schema does not list, is passed on
  - `update_points!(db, selection; phase = ...)` changes the phase of points that are already in a database of yours (a text, a vector of texts or a function; the empty text clears it); the template of Excel accepts a phase of your own (the menu is no longer strict) and offers the three saturation names; its ThermoML check uses the same translation

- Sorting points (issue 42): `table(v; sortby, rev)` sorts the points by a column (`sortby = :temperature`), by a pair with a direction (`:unc_k2 => :desc`) or by a list of keys (`[:property, :temperature => :desc]`), and `sort(t::PointTable; by, rev)`, `sort!` (in place: the order and one spare column at a time instead of a copy of the table) and `sortperm(t::PointTable; by, rev)` do it on a table that exists
  - The sort is stable and the points without a value for a key (`NaN`, empty text, an unknown year or number of digits) go last in both directions. `:relative_unc` (`unc_k2 / abs(value)`) is a key that is not a column; sorting by `value`, `unc` or `unc_k2` points of different units warns
  - When every key can be read from the data (`year`, `doi`, `source`, `property`, `method`, `phase`, `temperature`, `pressure`, `value`, `unc`, `unc_k`, `unc_level`, `unc_k2`, `relative_unc`) the rows are sorted before the columns are built, which needs less time and memory; with another key the table is built and then sorted, with the same result. A selection of several databases is sorted after its parts are joined (`database` is a key). The selection itself is not reordered, and `sort!` changes the columns of a table, never the database
- A shown table says how many of its `temperature`, `pressure`, `value`, `unc`, `unc_k2`, `method` and `phase` the source does not give (the line `missing` of the summary), and the points listed below mark them as blank cells

## [0.1.1] - 2026-11-21

- Links that were broken are fixed: the contributing page of the website (the address with a final slash is not served), the documentation of Unitful.jl (moved to JuliaPhysics) and the repository of FlexUnits.jl (moved to Deduction42), in the README, the manual and the issue templates.
- The installation instructions of the README and of the getting started page use the General registry (`Pkg.add("ThermophysicalDB")`) now that the package is registered; the GitHub address is kept for the development version.
- The link checker (`.lychee.toml`) retries a link that answers with a temporary error before failing, and leaves out the one article whose publisher refuses automatic requests.
- A download that stalls no longer holds the package for ever: the system `curl` gives up when no data arrives for 30 seconds (Julia's own downloader already did after 20), and the environment variable `THERMOPHYSICALDB_DOWNLOAD_TIMEOUT` limits the seconds of each try (no limit by default). The documentation build, which waited for hours for the NIST servers, now downloads the archive once, at the start, and stops there with the explanation when it cannot
- A download that the server interrupts is continued, not started again: what the system `curl` received is kept (`ThermoML.vYYYY-MM-DD.tgz.part.curl-part`, next to the archive) and the next try, in the same call or in a later one, asks only for the rest. The archive is still checked against the SHA-256 published by NIST before it is installed, and nothing is downloaded when the installed archive is the current one
- When NIST has a newer archive and it cannot be installed (the server stalls, or the file is not the one NIST published), `database(; update = :install)`, `ensure_archive(; update = :install)` and `update_database!()` keep the archive that is installed and warn, instead of failing; the next call tries again. With nothing installed the error is still thrown
- The documentation workflow keeps the last ThermoML archive that worked (and the database built from it) in the cache of the repository, installs or updates it in a step of its own with a time limit, and limits the job to one hour; a workflow of its own (`ArchiveCache`) restores it twice a week so that the cache does not expire, and puts it back when it is missing

## [0.1.0] - 2026-11-18

- Removing and emptying databases (issue 30, first stage): `remove_database!(db, name_or_names; force = false)` moves the folder of a saved database, with its audit log, to a folder of the temporary directory (the system deletes it in due course) and returns its path, so a backup is a copy of that folder; a database with unsaved points is refused unless `force = true`, the archive is never removed, and the event `database_removed` is logged. `clear_database!(db, name)` empties a database and keeps its name, kind, priority and creation time until the next `save_database`

- Removing references and points that are already there (issue 30, second stage): `remove_reference!(db, reference_or_doi; database)` removes one article with its experiments, points, samples, conditions and uncertainties and returns the counts; `add_reference_data!(...; on_conflict = :ask)` says what to do with new points that the database already has
  - "The same point" is the criterion of `duplicates` (same work, system and property, and conditions and value within the `DuplicateRules`, which can be given with `rules`); it is looked for with `find_repeats` after the points are added, only against what the database had before. A point that is not there is no conflict
  - `on_conflict` is `:ask` (default; asks in a terminal session and is an error that says what to do in a script, a test or a documentation build), `:skip` (leave out the new points that are already there), `:replace` (the old points go, the new ones stay), `:keep` (both stay, with the notice as before) or `:error`. A refused addition leaves nothing behind. `:replace` is recorded as `points_replaced`, a removal as `reference_removed` and `clear_database!` as `database_cleared` in the audit log at the next `save_database`
  - The rows are numbered again after a removal or a replacement, so a reference or experiment held from before is refused with a message instead of being used on other rows; get it again from the database. The other references keep every value

- Removing and changing points (issue 30, third stage): `remove_points!(db, selection; confirm = false)` and `update_points!(db, selection; value, temperature, pressure, unc, unc_k, unc_level, x1, x2, x3, on_conflict)` work on the result of `points`, so whatever finds points says which ones to remove or change
  - `remove_points!` only says what would go until `confirm = true` (`(points, experiments, databases, removed)`); an experiment left without points goes with them and the references stay. It makes a new dataset, so the rows are numbered again and the references, experiments and selections held before are refused
  - `update_points!` takes, for each column, a number (the same for all, `NaN` clears), a vector (one per point of the selection) or a function of the current number (`value = v -> v * 1.002`). Every new number is checked with the rules of an addition before anything is written; the digits that the source gave for a changed number are forgotten; nothing is renumbered. A change that makes a point repeat another (as `duplicates` says) is asked about, kept or refused with `on_conflict = :ask | :keep | :error`
  - A selection with points of the archive, or one that is out of date, is refused. `points_removed` and `points_updated` are written in the audit log at the next `save_database`. To add one point, `add_measurement!` to an experiment already exists and is now shown in the manual. The documentation now says, in the manual and in every function that changes data, that the changes are in memory until `save_database`

- Numbers are exported as they were typed, without the noise of binary arithmetic in the last digits (issue 32): `0.12229999999999999` and `6.115000000000001e-5` no longer appear in a file
  - A number that you give is converted from its decimal value and rounded once (`122.3` mW/m/K is `0.1223` W/m/K, and `"0.05 %"` of 0.1223 is `6.115e-5`); the unit catalog constants are correctly rounded (`Float64` of a large rational rounded twice before). In `table`, the columns that come from a multiplication (`value`, `unc`, `unc_k2`, `pressure`, a composition in another scale, a temperature or pressure in another unit) are rounded to 15 significant digits, and a column that is not converted is not touched
  - `export_points(...; report = :source)` (the default) writes the numbers as the ThermoML standard keeps them, with the digits of the source; `report = :gum` rounds as the GUM 7.2.6 asks: an uncertainty has at most two significant digits (two when the leading digit is 1 or 2, otherwise one) and the value is rounded to the last digit of the uncertainty at `k = 2`. Digits are only taken away, and a point without an uncertainty is not changed. The ThermoML formats never round
- `union`, `intersect` and `setdiff` combine selections of the same dataset (`DataView`) or database (`DatabaseView`), and `pure_component(source, component; tol = 1e-4, ...)` finds the points of a pure compound wherever they were measured (issue 31)
  - `∪` and `∩` (`\cup` and `\cap` and Tab) are the same functions as `union` and `intersect`, so `a ∪ b` and `a ∩ b` work; Julia has no symbol for `setdiff`. The three are documented in the reference and in a new section of the manual ("Combining selections"), with the use for a literature review: what two searches have in common, and what one adds to the other
  - `overlap(a, b, c...; names)` (or `overlap(:a => a, :b => b, ...)`) places the points of several selections (up to 16) in the regions that say which of them hold each point (only `a`, `a ∩ b`, all three, ...) in one pass, and counts the points and the references of each region: the table of what the searches share and what each one adds, as used in a literature review. The points of a region are a selection: `ov["a ∩ b"]` or `ov[2]`. It works on selections of a dataset and of a database (by member); the points that two databases hold both are different rows and count separately
  - The operations merge the sorted rows of the selections, linear in their length and without copying data; each point is in the result once, and the result is a selection like any other (refine it with `points`, give it to `table`). For a database, `union` leaves out the points that two databases hold both like `points` does (`duplicate = true` keeps them)
  - `pure_component` returns the systems of one component and the points of mixtures in which the compound is the whole system, the usual way an article reports a pure compound as the end of a table of compositions: its amount is a fraction (mole, mass) of at least `1 - tol` wherever it sits in the system (`x1`, `x2` or `x3`), or its amount is not given and the amounts of all the others are fractions that add up to at most `tol`. Molalities and concentrations never qualify, and a point whose composition does not tell is left out. Other keywords of `points` refine it; where a point came from is `setdiff(pure_component(db, c), points(db; component = c, pure = true))`

- `export_database(db, names, path)` and `import_database!(db, path; name, only, on_conflict, dir)` take databases of your own to another computer in one `.tdb` file (issue 26)
  - The file is a gzip-compressed tar (any tool that opens a `.tar.gz` opens it) with, for each database, `dataset.jld2`, `member.toml`, `audit.log` and `audit.toml`, and a `manifest.toml` with the SHA-256 of every file. Only the saved version is exported (a warning says when the memory has more); the archive is never exported
  - The audit log goes with the data, so merging is auditable: the export is recorded in the log of the source (`database_exported`) before the copy of the log is made; an import checks the digests, the schema version and the chain of hashes of the log before writing anything, and records `database_imported` in the log of the destination with the source log (`source`, `source_lines`, `source_head`) and the SHA-256 of the file (`bundle_sha256`). The log that came is kept whole in `imports/`, and each computer keeps a log of its own, so logs never fork
  - `on_conflict` is `:error` (default), `:rename` (`name-2`, ...) or `:replace` (refused if the database to replace has unsaved data). Databases that are imported side by side are combined by `points`, with `duplicates` and `conflicts` to compare them; there is no physical merge of two databases into one

- `database()` attaches the compound database by default (`compound_library = true`), so a compound can be named by any synonym, CAS number, PubChem CID, InChI or SMILES without the steps `compound_database()` and `link_compounds` (issue 23)
  - Nothing is read or downloaded by `database()` for this: the compound database is loaded the first time a name has to be understood, and a failure (no cache and no network) is a warning, once, after which names are understood the way the archive spells them. `compound_library = false` attaches nothing; a compound database (or a function that loads one) can be given instead
  - There is a single type: `LinkedDatabase` is gone and `link_compounds(db, cdb)` is now `link_compounds!(db, cdb)`, which attaches the compound database to `db` and returns it (`link_compounds(dataset, cdb)` returns a `Database` of that dataset). The table of links is `link_table(db)` (with the column `database`), made the first time it is looked at and again after the members change; `db.choices` keeps the answers to the menus
  - Every function that took a `LinkedDatabase` takes the `Database`: `points`, `mixture`, `find_compounds`, `lookup_compounds(db, text)` (no compound database to give), `prefetch_compounds!(db)`, `convert_composition` of a selection (the molar masses come from the attached compound database), and refining a selection, `points(view; component = "oxidane")`. A `component` or a `fraction` may also be what `lookup_compounds` or `find_compounds` returned (`records[1]` takes one row)
  - A member that lacks the compound is not read when the compound was named by `find_compounds` or a name that the catalogs know; the amounts of a `fraction` are translated to the identifiers of each member like the `component` (a compound given as the result of `find_compounds(db, ...)` was read with the identifiers of the first member in every member before)
  - A text that is not an exact name of a compound (a typo, a part of a name) lists the close compounds and asks which one is meant (an error that lists them when the session is not interactive); with `compound_library = false` it is an error with a suggestion

- A menu is asked only where a person can answer it: a terminal session whose output is the terminal. A documentation build, a test run or a script, which capture the output, get the `ArgumentError` that lists the candidates, instead of waiting forever for a key (issue 23)
  - A text with a single exact answer is remembered by the database until its members or compounds change, so `mixture` and repeated searches do not look it up again
  - The search for the compounds of a dataset reads the names of the compound database only when it found nothing (to suggest close names), and counts the points once

- The reference lists `audit_log`, `verify_audit_log` and `audit_checkpoint`, and the manual has a short section on the audit log (the documentation check warned that they were missing)

- `table` returns a `PointTable`, which wraps the same `NamedTuple` of columns (`parent(t)` gives it back; `t.temperature`, `keys(t)` and `DataFrame(t)` work as before) and is indexed like a data frame: `t[:temperature]` (the column), `t[3]` (a row, as a `NamedTuple`), `t[3, :temperature]` and `t[:temperature, 3]` (one value), `t[3, 2]` (by position), `t[1:5]`, `t[1:5, :temperature]`, `t[1:5, [:temperature, :value]]` and a `Bool` mask (copies of the rows). Iterating gives the rows. Showing it prints a summary (points, references and years, systems, ranges of the conditions and of the values of each property and unit) computed at that moment, then the first points

- Added `molar_mass(db, compound; unit = "g/mol", quantities = nothing)`: the molar mass of a compound of the compound database, found by any identifier of `lookup_compounds` (name, CAS number, InChIKey, formula, ...), by `id`, by a vector of `id`s or from the result of `lookup_compounds`. With `quantities = Unitful` (or `DynamicQuantities`, `FlexUnits`) it is a quantity, so that a molar property divided by it is a property per mass; `unit` is any unit of mass per amount of substance, converted exactly. A name that matches nothing exactly, no compound with a molar mass, or compounds that disagree on it is an `ArgumentError` that says what to do

- Audit log of the permanent changes to the data on disk (the archive that is installed, the databases that are saved, their priority, the compounds that are saved):
  - A plain text `audit.log` next to what it describes, one line per event, with the time (UTC), the event, the object, the SHA-256 of the file and the counts before and after; no user or host name is recorded.
  - Every line has a hash of the line before it, so an edit from outside breaks the chain: `verify_audit_log(db)` says which line fails, and finds a log that was removed or replaced (each log has a UUID, kept in `audit.toml`).
  - Operations have a start and an end (`database_save_started`, then `database_saved`), so one that was cut short shows as a start with no end; an error gives `database_save_failed` (also for the compounds and the archive) with the error, and is thrown as before. `archive_rejected` records an archive whose SHA-256 does not match, and `database_unreadable` a saved database that cannot be read (once, however many sessions find it). Errors of use and network failures are not recorded.
  - `audit_checkpoint(db; database)` gives the state of a log to write down elsewhere; `verify_audit_log(db; database, checkpoint)` then proves that the log was not rewritten.
  - `audit_log(db; database, since, event)` reads the events as a table. Changes that only live in memory are not recorded.
  - New dependencies of the standard library: FileWatching (a lock while a line is written) and UUIDs.
- Continuous integration: `THERMOPHYSICALDB_TEST_SUITE` chooses what `test/runtests.jl` runs (`full` by default); Linux runs everything and is the only one that instruments for coverage, macOS everything but the Aqua and JET checks, Windows only the files that depend on the system
- `points` has new filters, and two functions list their values:
  - `phase` and `method`: text that the phase or the method contains (any case), a `Regex` or a vector. `phases(source)` and `measurement_methods(source)` list what a dataset, a selection or a database have, with the number of points.
  - `fraction = "methane" => (0.2, 0.4)`: the amount of a compound, found wherever it sits in the system (a vector of pairs must all hold); `xkind` limits the kind of composition (`"mole"`, `"mass"`).
  - `value` and `uncertainty` (at k = 2): limits in the native unit of each property, or in another unit with `unit = "mW/m/K"` or with quantities of a units package, which leaves out the properties of another dimension.
  - `relative_uncertainty`: the uncertainty at k = 2 as a fraction of the value, for any property.
  - The limits of a ratio (`relative_uncertainty` and the limits of `fraction`) may be dimensionless quantities such as `2u"percent"` or `500u"ppm"`; the exact unit parser now reads `%`, `percent`, `permille`, `‰`, `ppm` and `ppb`.
  - The filters are built once per call (a mask over the small pool of phases or methods, limits for each property, factors for the coverage factors that the data pool), so a row costs a few array lookups and a call without them pays one test per row: 2 million points are filtered in 7 to 25 ms, and only the vector of rows is allocated.
- Code complexity is checked by the tests (tag `:quality`, so on Linux only, with Aqua and JET): CodeComplexity.jl measures the cyclomatic complexity, the cognitive complexity and the number of arguments of every function of `src`, and a function above the limit fails the test and is listed with its file and line. The limits are, for now, the largest values the package has (84, 131 and 22), so nothing gets worse; they are to be lowered as the most complex functions (`run_detection`, `thermoml_experiment`, `experiment_arguments`, `find_outliers`) are split
- The search for repeated points keeps the key of each work (DOI, first author, year, title) of a reference table once it is made (`WorkKeys`, not stored in the file), instead of building it again at every `points` call; it saves about a third of the allocations of a filter over a database that has a database of yours
- Fewer allocations, measured with the study of allocations on the real archive and checked bit for bit: the rows of a `DataView` are a `UnitRange{Int32}` when they are consecutive (`DataView(dataset)` and the first filter of a database no longer allocate a vector of every row, 21 MiB for the archive), and a filter no longer reserves room for as many rows as it scans; the trend stage of `find_outliers` fits its windows in a workspace that each thread keeps (`LineWS`), which allocates 65 % less in the same time, with the same numbers; the spreadsheet template writes the references of its cells straight into the bytes of the string
- Added a study of allocations by hand (`benchmark/allocations/`): scenarios for the filters, units, tables, composition, duplicates, compounds, uncertainty, export, template and outliers over synthetic or real data, with time, bytes, allocations, allocation by line (`Profile.Allocs`), JET reports, and fingerprints of the results that check that a performance change does not change the data
- Fixed the tests and the unit text on macOS: Unitful prints exponents as superscripts there (and with `UNITFUL_FANCY_EXPONENTS=true`), which the exact unit parser does not read, so the units of a Unitful quantity are now asked for with `^` exponents on every system; the CI matrix is Julia 1.13 on Linux, Windows and macOS for now

- `points(db; ...)` leaves out the repeated points by default (`duplicate = false`; `duplicate = true` shows every point), also the points of an experiment that repeat an earlier experiment of the same database of yours (the same file imported again); they stay in the database, replicates inside one experiment are kept, and `add_reference_data!` warns about them

- Added `export_points`, which writes the points of a selection to a directory (created if needed), one file per format chosen with `formats`, a tuple of symbols: `:csv`, `:md`, `:tex`, `:json`, `:jld2`, `:xlsx` (added to the default when XLSX.jl is loaded) `:thermoml` (the JSON form of ThermoML) and `:thermoml_xml` (ThermoML XML); the references table and the `.bib` are written next to the table formats, and a path with a file extension is an error

- Units are now package extensions: Unitful, DynamicQuantities and FlexUnits (FlexUnits is experimental) are weak dependencies, and `table(v; quantities = Unitful)` (or `DynamicQuantities`, `FlexUnits`) returns columns with units; there is no other keyword: without `quantities` the columns are plain `Float64`, and the module of the package you pass (`Unitful`, `DynamicQuantities`, `FlexUnits`) chooses how the quantities come out. Limits of `points` and the units of `reference_summary` accept quantities of any loaded backend. The data stay `Float64` in the units of the archive; quantities exist only at the boundary
- Unit conversions are exact: every factor and offset is a rational (`Rational{Int128}`) derived from the legal definition of the unit (inch, pound, standard gravity, rankine, ...), the factors of the unit catalog are derived from the unit strings and checked for dimension, and a chain of conversions is composed in rationals and rounded to `Float64` once. Conversions of a column are a single pass (identity, scale, or scale and offset)
- `table(v; unit = ...)` gives the columns `value`, `unc` and `unc_k2` in any unit of the same quantity, for a selection with one unit. The unit is text, read with exact rational arithmetic (SI prefixes and compound units: `"mW/m/K"`, `"mbar"`, `"Btu/hr/ft/degF"`, with Btu, calorie, hr, min, L, gal, P, St, lb, lbf, ft, in, psi, ...), or a unit object of Unitful, DynamicQuantities or FlexUnits, which also returns quantities of that package; the dimensions are checked. A new function `unit_factor` completes the protocol of the extensions
- `table(v; temperature_unit = ..., pressure_unit = ...)` gives the columns `temperature` and `pressure` in any unit of those quantities, by name (`"degC"`, `"degF"`, `"mK"`, `"bar"`, `"mbar"`, `"psi"`, `"N/m2"`) or as a unit object; the conversion is exact, with the offset of degC and degF. When a unit object comes from a units package, its text is read by the exact parser of this package and, if it is understood, the whole conversion is done here and the package only receives the column; otherwise the package converts. The unit reader accepts spaces, `^`, parentheses and `·`, as the units packages print units (`"mW m^-1 K^-1"`, `"W/(m*K)"`). The extensions may add `unit_text` (optional)
- Every unit conversion goes through the exact arithmetic: also the quantities that the user gives (limits of `points`, values and uncertainties, `reference_summary`) are converted here, with the exact value of the number and one rounding, when the units package gives the unit as text that is understood; the package converts only a unit whose symbol is not known. The names of a temperature scale are aliases of one definition (`°C`, `degC`, `℃`; `°F`, `degF`, `℉`; `°R`, `degR`, `Ra`), and a property that is a temperature (in kelvin) can be asked in `degC` or `degF`, with the offset for the value and the scale only for the uncertainty. A quantity with a wrong dimension is an `ArgumentError` for every package of units. The extensions implement `quantity_parts` (optional) and `package_native_value`, `package_native_difference` and `package_convert_native` instead of the old `native_value`, `native_difference` and `convert_native`
- The terms of `set_uncertainty!` accept a quantity of any units package, a text with a number and a unit (`"0.5 mW/m/K"`, `"5 mbar"`), and a relative uncertainty (`"2 %"`, `"500 ppm"`, `2u"percent"`, a fraction of the value of the same quantity at the point); they are converted as differences into the unit of the database with the exact arithmetic of the package, and `UncertaintyTerm` keeps the value as given until it is stored. A quantity with a wrong dimension is an `ArgumentError` for every package
- Reduced the memory of the database by about 38 % (516 to 317 MB for 2.78 million measurements): `unc_k`, `unc_level` and the new `assessment` are stored as one byte per row pointing into their few distinct values (`PooledFloats`, same values on reading), the identifier columns of the measurements use the narrowest unsigned integer, the evaluator and the assessment number of the uncertainty moved from the extras table (5.5 million repeated rows) to columns of the measurements, the extras that are the same for every point of a measured property are stored once for the whole block (`:block` scope, `block` column) and still read for every point by `extra_numbers` and `extra_texts`, and a freshly built database releases the spare capacity of its columns. The cache schema version is now 8, so the cache is rebuilt once
- Added `convert_composition` and the keyword `composition` of `table` and `export_points`: the composition of a selection is expressed in one scale (mole fraction, mass fraction, molality, amount or mass ratio, moles per mass of solution, molarity or mass concentration) whatever scale each source used. The molar masses are the ones you give, then the compound database, and with `fetch_molar_mass = true` PubChem; the solvent and the density of the solution are given by you and never guessed, and a point that cannot be converted keeps `NaN` with the reason in `composition_status`. The data of the archive are not changed. `compound_links` accepts `only` to link some compounds.
- A database read from the cache file now reads its large tables only when something uses them (lazy loading, `LAZY_FIELDS`: samples, purity steps, extra fields, conditions and the text pool of the extras), so `database()` followed by `points`, `table` and the other queries of the core tables keeps about a quarter less in memory and reads the file about four times faster once compiled (0.47 s instead of about 2 s for 2.78 million measurements). `loaded_tables(ds)` says what is in memory, `validate` and the cache writer read what is missing, and a cache file that was replaced after the database was read is refused with a message to load it again. The cache format is unchanged.
- `database()` now returns a `Database`: independent databases (`members`) that look like one. The ThermoML archive is the member `thermoml`; points of the user (`:user`) and from the literature (`:literature`) are separate members with the same tables, each in its own file, with a priority (default archive, literature, user) for a repeated point. A registry of global numbers for compounds, properties and references, always in memory, maps the numbers of each member to the global ones and, from a small catalog of each file, tells which members a query can find something in: only those are read. `points(db; ...)` returns a `DatabaseView` with one `DataView` per database that has points (`parts`), `table` joins them (column `database`), `find_compounds(db, text)` joins the compounds, and a database with one member behaves as the dataset did. Export, outliers and the reference tables work on one database for now.
